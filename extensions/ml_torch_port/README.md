# Extension: the numpy networks in PyTorch

**This is our extension, not part of the P24 / P25 reproduction.** The machine-learning code of
[ml_design_assist](../ml_design_assist/README.md) is written by hand in numpy so that every step can be read. This
extension writes the same networks in PyTorch and compares the two, for two reasons:

- **A second implementation.** PyTorch's autograd derives the gradients that `ml_cnn`'s backward methods write out
  by hand. Agreement to rounding confirms both.
- **Speed and common practice.** Later deep-learning work can use a standard framework if the numbers carry over.

**Status (2026-10-12): A199-A204 done.**
- **A199, the CNN port (6 of 6).** With the same weights and inputs in float64, outputs agree within 5.3e-15,
  autograd agrees with the hand-written backward within 7.6e-15, and 20 Adam steps stay within 6.7e-08. Retrained
  on A146's data, the test errors are 0.93-1.00 of A146's on average.
- **A200, the audit (7 of 7).** Every other hand-written gradient agrees with autograd to rounding: the MLP and
  its training loop, A147's autoencoder layers, the PPO actor and critic including the anchored policy, and
  REINFORCE. A150's Gaussian-process fits, optimised with finite-difference gradients, had converged.
- **A201, ten seeds and an ensemble (5 of 5).** A146's test medians span L 1.9-2.8 %, Q_act 3.2-4.4 %, di/dt
  5.3-6.8 %, k 0.0113-0.0122 over ten seeds; its criterion 4 passes on 7 of 10. The mean of the ten networks gives
  1.26 / 1.94 / 4.13 % / 0.0102 with covers at 0.90, and is the "CNN" step from now on. Outside the training
  conditions it is more overconfident than one network.
- **A202, design search by gradients (3 of 4).** Backpropagation to the inputs of A120's surrogate finds a smaller
  worst peak than 100 000 random designs (191.05 against 193.67 A; 192.86 A at equal cost) and a larger front.
  The surrogate's optimum does not hold in D63 (198.8-200.1 A): near the feasible corner its error (+-7-9 A)
  exceeds what the search optimises. A surrogate optimum is never quoted without a D63 re-check.
- **A203, the search closed with D63 (4 of 4).** Ten rounds of search, 16 designs to D63, fine-tuning: 189.88 A
  in 160 D63 designs, against 190.33 A for the best of 100 000 random D63 designs (random D63 at equal cost: median
  196.3 A). D63 itself finds 1530 feasible designs where A120's surrogate said 240 (precision 0.40, recall 0.06);
  the surrogate's bulk statistics (21 % feasible at bus slew >= 5 us) hold. A surrogate search is closed with D63.
- **A204, the ten networks' disagreement as a guard (2 of 4).** Calibrated inside the training conditions (4.7 %
  flagged, cover 0.90), blind outside them (35 % flagged at 0.8 V noise, 10 % at a 0.5 GHz probe, 6 % at I0 +5 %):
  the networks share their bias. Not adopted; A146's measurement requirements stay external.
- Not done: A146 retrained on wider measurement conditions; an input-space guard (noise level and rise time read
  off the capture); PPO retrained in PyTorch (the environment is numpy, and RL was not adopted).

**The rest of the repository stays numpy only.** `torch` is not in `requirements.txt`, nothing outside this folder
imports `torch_cnn.py`, and the portable test suite does not touch it. That is why the module is here and not in
`src/scb_ivr/extensions/`.

**To run:** a separate Python environment with `torch`, `numpy` and `scipy` (A199 used torch 2.14.1 on Python 3.12),
then from the project root with `PYTHONPATH=src`:

```
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A199_cnn_torch_port/a199_checks.py
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A199_cnn_torch_port/a199_train.py --seed 0
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A199_cnn_torch_port/a199_analyze.py
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A200_autograd_audit/a200_audit.py
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A202_surrogate_inverse_design/a202_inverse.py
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A203_surrogate_refinement_loop/a203_loop.py
python extensions/ml_torch_port/experiments/A204_ensemble_spread_guard/a204_spread.py      # numpy only
```

On the machine used here float32 on the CPU is about 5x slower than float64 (not traced); float32 runs use
`--device mps`.

The dataset is A146's (`tmp/a146/traces.npz`, regenerated by A146's `a146_data.py`).
