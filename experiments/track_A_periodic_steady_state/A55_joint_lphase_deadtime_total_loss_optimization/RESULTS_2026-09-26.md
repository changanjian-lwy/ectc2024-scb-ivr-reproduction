# A55: local four-phase ZVS boundary and branch-power audit

## Question and controlled change

Compared with `asymmetric_baseline_points.json`, vary ONLY phase inductance
between the re-solved margin and old-critical points. Keep dead time 2.15 ns,
48 V input, 5 MHz, four phases, one simulated module, 4 mOhm load, 3 uF flying
capacitors, and EPC2067 population-corrected capacitance/resistance fixed.
This is a sensitivity extension, not native P24/P25 reproduction or zero start.

The inherited controller has fixed PWM windows with zero-voltage admission
inside those windows; it does not enforce P24's 1–2% negative-current turn-off
rule. The output is a dynamic capacitor/resistor load, not an ideal 1 V clamp.
Three isolated divider coordinates are pinned for the fast periodic solve;
the physical flying-capacitor voltages and all four currents are solved.

## Boundary result

Two endpoints were replayed, followed by seven local bisections. Each point
was independently re-solved to the inherited relative closure tolerance 1e-6;
no unconverged or over-250 A trial was accepted. The two final endpoints were
then re-solved at 31.25 ps normal / 2.5 ps commutation steps instead of
62.5 ps / 5 ps. The classifications remained unchanged.

The transition is locally bracketed, not a global monotonicity proof or an
optimal design. The +/-250 A threshold is a project screen, not device SOA.
All Newton/line-search probes and final-orbit peaks are distinguished in the
JSON. The apparent numerical precision must not be read as component accuracy.

At the passing point the four minimum currents are
`-79.712/-80.523/-80.521/-80.540 A`; maxima are
`205.870/204.952/204.953/204.341 A`. Negative valleys are approximately
39% of their respective positive peaks. This does not demonstrate the paper's
small-negative-current control condition, and output power is still below
250 W. A local ZVS pass is therefore not full reproduction success.

## Discovered measurement error and correction

The old `path_resolved_period.py` separates high/low/dead-time intervals but
uses each phase's inductor current in every assigned switch path. In this
series-capacitor topology, charge-transfer current also enters those paths;
the channel current is not generally equal to that inductor current.

`audit_accepted_orbit.py` independently calculates the enabled resistive
channel power from the actual node voltages:

    P_channel = (1/T) sum_switches integral_enabled (v_branch^2/R_branch) dt
    P_load = (1/T) integral (Vout^2/Rload) dt

It removes provisional post-crossing samples when the event solver rolls back
to a refined crossing. This is a measurement change only: the periodic map,
topology, parameters and event physics are unchanged. A separate test confirms
branch dissipation equals the enabled-channel conductance-matrix quadratic form
for all four high-side positions.

At the old margin point the old proxy was 25.259 W and actual model channel
dissipation is 28.910 W. At the newly bracketed passing point the corresponding
values are 21.718 W and 24.955 W. The old values are retained as historical
proxies, not silently replaced as if they were original branch measurements.

Third-quadrant physical conduction remains represented by an ideal admission
surrogate; the metered channel power is only that of the implemented resistor
model. Gate drive, realistic reverse conduction, magnetic and thermal losses
remain incomplete. No total-efficiency or hardware result is claimed.
